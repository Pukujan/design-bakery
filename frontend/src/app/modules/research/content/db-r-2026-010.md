# Comparing Jev with contemporary model judges on objective decision tasks

**Status:** Working paper; benchmark results through EXP-025 complete; pending owner review<br />
**Experiment family:** EXP-013, EXP-014, EXP-019, EXP-022, EXP-024, and EXP-025<br />
**Repository:** [Eval Lab on GitHub](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab/tree/task/TASK-0041-grok-protocol-ablation)

## Abstract

We compared Jev with Qwen Flash and Grok 4.6 and 4.7 on a frozen 760-record blind holdout, then evaluated eight model routes on the same records. The full pool contains 1,408 objective-decision records with fixed rubrics, closed label sets, and gold labels from trusted benchmark answer keys or deterministic verifiers. We report conditional accuracy and coverage so unresolved responses remain visible.

Jev resolved all 760 blind records at 89.87% accuracy. Qwen Flash reached 97.36% accuracy at 99.87% coverage; Grok 4.6 and 4.7 reached 43.46% and 43.53%, respectively. In the extended eight-route comparison, Qwen3.8 Flash had the highest accuracy among fully covered routes (99.21%); DeepSeek V4 Flash had the highest conditional accuracy (99.84%) at 80.39% coverage. The preregistered Grok ablation found no tested protocol that improved on the selected typed baseline. For local Qwen 4B, split-safe temperature scaling improved Brier score, negative log likelihood, and expected calibration error while preserving predicted labels.

On this objective pool, Jev paired complete coverage with lower accuracy than the strongest evaluated Qwen systems. The findings concern typed objective decisions; open-ended legal and financial reasoning was outside the study's scope.

## 1. Introduction

This study compares Jev with contemporary model judges on objective decisions. We use a fixed record set, explicit rubrics, a closed label space, and independently established gold labels to assess each system against the same target.

We ask how Jev performs relative to Qwen, Grok, and other strong model judges when all systems receive the same typed decision packet and are scored on the same source-problem-disjoint blind holdout. The pool includes single decisions labeled `pass` or `fail` and pairwise decisions labeled `A`, `B`, or `TIE`. We measure accuracy and coverage, then assess probability calibration for the comparison arm with a validated distribution over labels.

The study makes four contributions:

1. It reports a matched comparison of Jev, Grok, and Qwen on a frozen 760-record blind holdout.
2. It reports an eight-route comparison on the same holdout, with conditional accuracy and coverage shown separately.
3. It evaluates four preregistered Grok task and output protocols using a public diagnostic and blind rerun.
4. It measures split-safe confidence calibration for a local Qwen 4B judge.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Evaluation pool and gold labels

The frozen pool contains 1,408 records: 648 public-selection records and 760 source-problem-disjoint blind-holdout records. Each record contains a question, one or two candidate answers, a rubric, a closed set of permitted labels, and an objective gold label. Single records use `pass` or `fail`; pairwise records use `A`, `B`, or `TIE`.

Gold labels come from trusted benchmark answer keys or deterministic verifiers. We score each model output against those labels and supply the same typed packet to every comparison arm. A response counts as resolved when it matches a permitted label. Accuracy is the fraction correct among resolved responses; coverage is the resolved fraction of all records. The experiment artifacts distinguish parse failures from service interruptions.

### 2.2 Comparison arms

The direct comparison includes Jev 1.13, Qwen Flash, Grok 4.6 Build, and Grok 4.7 Build. The extended blind comparison contains eight model routes: Qwen3.8 Flash, Qwen 3.8 Max, GLM 5.2, Kimi K2.7 Code, DeepSeek V4 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, GLM 5.3 Flash, and MiniMax M3. Both waves use the same blind holdout and are reported separately as preregistered comparison arms.

The local Qwen 4B arm belongs to the calibration study. It produces a probability map over the legal labels and is therefore evaluated with probability-sensitive metrics in addition to accuracy.

### 2.3 Grok protocol ablation

EXP-025 preregistered four Grok contracts: the typed schema used in the primary comparison, an explicit task with schema, semantic labels with schema, and an explicit task without a native schema. The public diagnostic compared these contracts before the blind rerun; an alternative had to improve the score meaningfully while maintaining at least 95% coverage.

After a Windows locale-decoding failure, the no-schema arm resumed with UTF-8 replacement enabled; EXP-025 preserves the normalized checkpoint and record-level statuses.

### 2.4 Calibration

For local Qwen 4B, we fit scalar temperature separately for single and pairwise records using public-selection labels, froze the parameters, and evaluated them on the blind holdout. Blind labels were reserved for evaluation. We report Brier score, negative log likelihood (NLL), and expected calibration error (ECE) before and after scaling.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Jev in the direct comparison

Jev resolved all 760 blind records and reached 89.87% accuracy. Qwen Flash produced 759 resolved labels and reached 97.36% accuracy. Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.7 produced 757 and 726 resolved labels, with accuracies of 43.46% and 43.53%.

| Judge | Resolved | Coverage | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen Flash | 759/760 | 99.87% | 97.36% |
| Jev | 760/760 | 100.00% | 89.87% |
| Grok 4.7 Build | 726/760 | 95.53% | 43.53% |
| Grok 4.6 Build | 757/760 | 99.61% | 43.46% |

Jev and Qwen agreed on 89.9% of comparable blind records. Grok 4.6 agreed with Jev on 41.5% of comparable records and with Qwen on 44.7%; Grok 4.7 agreed with Jev on 41.7% and with Qwen on 44.8%. Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.7 agreed on approximately 95.3% of comparable records. Their accuracies were nearly identical (43.46% and 43.53%), while Grok 4.7 had lower coverage (95.53% versus 99.61%).

### 3.3 Variation across domains

The multidomain holdout places the objective-pool results in context. Qwen blind accuracy was 65.5% on GSM8K, 84.2% on professional law, 79.5% on machine learning, and 99.2% on the Eval Lab objective pool. Small synthetic subsets ranged from 20% to 100%. The differences indicate strong dependence on task and protocol.

Source: [EXP-013 multidomain report](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab/blob/task/TASK-0041-grok-protocol-ablation/experiments/EXP-20260921-013-qwen-multidomain-holdout/report.md).

### 3.4 Grok protocol ablation

The Grok result remained stable across EXP-019 and EXP-022. Grok returned valid structured responses at high coverage in the selected typed protocol, while accuracy remained low. The blind typed rerun resolved 757 of 760 records, with a mode-balanced score of 0.2586. Single-record accuracy was 0.4985 over 650 of 652 resolved records; pairwise accuracy was 0.0187 over 107 of 108 resolved records.

The typed contract remained selected after the public diagnostic: it resolved 64 of 64 records with a mode-balanced score of 0.2656. The explicit-schema arm resolved 63 of 64 with a score of 0.2581, and the semantic-schema arm resolved 63 of 64 with a score of 0.2661. The no-schema arm returned no valid labels; EXP-025 reports its parse, service, and rate-limit outcomes separately.

Grok Build scored poorly on the tested typed-judge protocol, and the task and schema variants did not materially improve its score. These results apply to the preregistered protocol family.

### 3.5 Confidence calibration

Calibration metrics are reported for local Qwen 4B, whose probability map was evaluated directly. Temperature scaling preserved predicted labels and accuracy while improving blind Brier score from 0.7037 to 0.5160, NLL from 1.0767 to 0.7430, and ECE from 0.3411 to 0.0783. We fit the calibration parameters on public records and applied them once to the blind holdout.

## 4. Discussion

Jev achieved complete coverage and 89.87% accuracy in the direct comparison. Qwen Flash reached 97.36% accuracy at 99.87% coverage. In the extended wave, Qwen3.8 Flash combined 99.21% accuracy with complete coverage; DeepSeek V4 Flash reached 99.84% conditional accuracy at 80.39% coverage. These results place Jev as a high-coverage, lower-accuracy comparator on this task family.

The two cross-model waves used distinct preregistered model sets, so we report their shared blind-pool results separately.

The Grok ablation tested whether task wording and output schema explained the low score. The selected baseline retained high coverage, and the tested alternatives did not materially improve performance. The low score recurred across the tested protocol family; the experiments estimate performance for those variants but cannot separate model behavior from harness effects.

Temperature scaling improved Qwen 4B's probability metrics while preserving its labels. Calibration claims are limited to this arm, which produced a validated probability map over the permitted labels.

## 5. Limitations

- The evidence covers objective typed decisions; open-ended legal, financial, human-preference, and expert-adjudicated tasks were not evaluated.
- Accuracy is conditional on a resolved label. Coverage and the experiment artifacts report unresolved execution and parsing outcomes.
- The extended-wave coverage reflects the EXP-024 parser and execution protocol. Grok conclusions apply to the preregistered task and schema variants.

## 6. Conclusion

This study compared Jev with Grok, Qwen, and other contemporary model judges on a frozen pool of objectively labeled decisions. Jev reached 89.87% accuracy with complete blind coverage. Qwen Flash achieved higher accuracy in the direct comparison. In the extended wave, Qwen3.8 Flash reached 99.21% accuracy and Qwen 3.8 Max reached 99.08%, both at complete coverage. Grok scored substantially lower under the tested typed-judge protocol, and none of the preregistered variants improved on the selected baseline.

These results support further evaluation of Jev on external objective benchmarks with deterministic verification, using matched records, explicit labels, coverage reporting, and validated confidence outputs.
