# Jev among contemporary model judges: comparative evaluation on frozen objective-decision tasks

**Status:** Working paper; benchmark results through EXP-025 complete; pending owner review<br />
**Experiment family:** EXP-013, EXP-014, EXP-019, EXP-022, EXP-024, and EXP-025<br />
**Repository:** [Eval Lab on GitHub](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab/tree/task/TASK-0041-grok-protocol-ablation)

## Abstract

We compared Jev with Qwen, Grok, and other high-performing model judges on a frozen pool of 1,408 objective-decision records. Each record used a fixed rubric, a closed set of permitted labels, and a gold label derived from a trusted answer key or deterministic verifier. The primary comparison held the records, rubrics, labels, and blind split constant while changing the judge. We report conditional accuracy together with execution coverage, and retain unresolved outputs as unresolved observations.

In the direct comparison, Jev resolved all 760 blind records and reached 89.87% accuracy. Qwen Flash reached 97.36% accuracy at 99.87% coverage, while Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.7 reached 43.46% and 43.53% accuracy, respectively. In the extended eight-model comparison, Qwen3.8 Flash combined 99.21% accuracy with complete coverage; DeepSeek V4 Flash reached the highest conditional accuracy, 99.84%, with 80.39% coverage. A preregistered Grok protocol ablation found no replacement protocol that improved the selected typed baseline. A separate local Qwen 4B study showed that split-safe temperature scaling improved Brier score, negative log likelihood, and expected calibration error while leaving labels unchanged.

The results position Jev as a reliable high-coverage judge on this objective pool, with lower accuracy than the strongest Qwen systems evaluated here. They also show that model comparisons depend on the task protocol and that confidence calibration requires a validated probability interface. The study does not measure general legal or financial reasoning.

## 1. Introduction

Comparing model judges requires a fixed task definition and a stable scoring target. Objective decision tasks provide a focused setting for comparison: a fixed record set, explicit rubric, closed label space, and independently established gold label make it possible to assess each judge against the same target.

This study asks how Jev performs relative to Qwen, Grok, and other strong model judges when all systems receive the same typed decision packet and are evaluated on the same source-problem-disjoint blind holdout. The comparison covers single decisions with `pass` or `fail` labels and pairwise decisions with `A`, `B`, or `TIE` labels. It examines three related properties: correctness, ability to return a usable decision, and the quality of a judge's probability interface when probabilities are available.

The study makes four contributions:

1. It reports a matched comparison of Jev, Grok, and Qwen on a frozen 760-record blind holdout.
2. It extends the comparison to eight additional models evaluated on the same holdout and separates conditional accuracy from coverage.
3. It tests whether alternative Grok task and output protocols change the observed result through a preregistered public diagnostic and blind rerun.
4. It evaluates split-safe confidence calibration for a local Qwen 4B judge.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Evaluation pool and gold labels

The frozen pool contains 1,408 records: 648 public-selection records and 760 source-problem-disjoint blind-holdout records. Each record contains a question, one or two candidate answers, a rubric, a closed set of permitted labels, and an objective gold label. Single records use `pass` or `fail`; pairwise records use `A`, `B`, or `TIE`.

Gold labels come from trusted benchmark answer keys or deterministic verifiers. Model outputs are predictions and are never used to define the gold standard. The same typed packet is supplied to each comparison arm. A prediction is resolved when it can be parsed into the legal label set. Accuracy is calculated over resolved labels, and coverage is the resolved fraction of all records. Parse failures and service interruptions remain distinct statuses in the underlying artifacts.

### 2.2 Comparison arms

The direct comparison includes Jev 1.13, Qwen Flash, Grok 4.6 Build, and Grok 4.7 Build. The extended blind comparison includes Qwen3.8 Flash, Qwen 3.8 Max, GLM 5.2, Kimi K2.7 Code, DeepSeek V4 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, GLM 5.3 Flash, and MiniMax M3. These waves are reported separately because they were preregistered as distinct comparison arms.

The local Qwen 4B arm belongs to the calibration study. It produces a probability map over the legal labels and is therefore evaluated with probability-sensitive metrics in addition to accuracy.

### 2.3 Grok protocol ablation

EXP-025 preregistered four Grok contracts: the typed schema used in the primary comparison, an explicit task with schema, semantic labels with schema, and an explicit task without a native schema. A public diagnostic selected the protocol for the blind rerun. The gate required a meaningful score improvement while maintaining at least 95% coverage.

One no-schema execution exposed a Windows locale-decoding defect in the shared stream reader. The reader was repaired to use explicit UTF-8 replacement, and the arm was resumed from its normalized checkpoint. The repair and all resulting statuses remain in the EXP-025 artifacts.

### 2.4 Calibration

For local Qwen 4B, scalar temperature scaling was fit separately for single and pairwise records using public-selection labels only. The fitted temperatures were then frozen before evaluation on the blind holdout. The blind labels were not used during calibration. Brier score, negative log likelihood (NLL), and expected calibration error (ECE) were calculated before and after scaling.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Jev in the direct comparison

Jev resolved all 760 blind records and reached 89.87% accuracy. Qwen Flash produced 759 resolved labels and reached 97.36% accuracy. Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.7 produced 757 and 726 resolved labels, with accuracies of 43.46% and 43.53%.

| Judge | Resolved | Coverage | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen Flash | 759/760 | 99.87% | 97.36% |
| Jev | 760/760 | 100.00% | 89.87% |
| Grok 4.7 Build | 726/760 | 95.53% | 43.53% |
| Grok 4.6 Build | 757/760 | 99.61% | 43.46% |

Jev and Qwen agreed on 89.9% of comparable blind records. Grok 4.6 agreed with Jev on 41.5% of comparable records and with Qwen on 44.7%; Grok 4.7 agreed with Jev on 41.7% and with Qwen on 44.8%. Grok 4.6 and Grok 4.7 agreed with each other on approximately 95.3% of comparable records; the later model version did not improve accuracy and had lower coverage.

### 3.3 Variation across domains

The multidomain holdout places the objective-pool results in context. Qwen blind accuracy was 65.5% on GSM8K, 84.2% on professional law, 79.5% on machine learning, and 99.2% on the Eval Lab objective pool. Small synthetic subsets ranged from 20% to 100%. The differences indicate strong dependence on task and protocol.

Source: [EXP-013 multidomain report](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab/blob/task/TASK-0041-grok-protocol-ablation/experiments/EXP-20260921-013-qwen-multidomain-holdout/report.md).

### 3.4 Grok protocol ablation

The Grok result remained stable across EXP-019 and EXP-022. Grok returned valid structured responses at high coverage in the selected typed protocol, while accuracy remained low. The blind typed rerun resolved 757 of 760 records, with a mode-balanced score of 0.2586. Single-record accuracy was 0.4985 over 650 of 652 resolved records; pairwise accuracy was 0.0187 over 107 of 108 resolved records.

The public diagnostic did not select an alternative protocol. The typed arm resolved 64 of 64 records with a mode-balanced score of 0.2656. The explicit-schema arm resolved 63 of 64 with a score of 0.2581, and the semantic-schema arm resolved 63 of 64 with a score of 0.2661. The no-schema arm produced no resolved labels after the reader repair and retained parse errors and service or rate-limit failures.

The ablation therefore supports a bounded conclusion: Grok Build underperformed in the tested typed-judge protocol, and the tested task and schema changes did not produce a materially better protocol. The result describes this protocol family and does not estimate Grok's general capability.

### 3.5 Confidence calibration

The external label-only judges in the comparison did not expose validated native probability maps. Their accuracy and agreement therefore do not provide calibration evidence.

For local Qwen 4B, temperature scaling left labels and accuracy unchanged and improved blind Brier score from 0.7037 to 0.5160, NLL from 1.0767 to 0.7430, and ECE from 0.3411 to 0.0783. The calibration parameters were fit on public records and applied once to the blind holdout.

Source: [EXP-019 calibration study](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab/blob/task/TASK-0041-grok-protocol-ablation/experiments/EXP-20260921-019-calibrated-judge-study/report.md).

## 4. Discussion

The direct comparison places Jev between Qwen and Grok on accuracy while giving Jev complete blind coverage. Jev's 89.87% accuracy shows strong performance on the frozen objective pool, although Qwen Flash reached a higher score with nearly complete coverage. In the extended comparison, Qwen3.8 Flash had the highest accuracy among systems with complete coverage, while DeepSeek V4 Flash recorded the highest conditional accuracy (99.84%) and resolved 80.39% of records. Qwen 3.8 Max also reached about 99% accuracy with complete coverage, and GLM 5.2 and Kimi K2.7 Code combined high accuracy with high coverage. Together, these results position Jev as a high-coverage comparator and capable judge for this task family, while showing that several evaluated models achieved substantially higher accuracy on the same frozen holdout.

The two cross-model waves should remain separate in interpretation. They were designed as distinct experiments and have different model sets and execution contexts. The paper reports their common blind-pool results side by side without constructing a pooled leaderboard.

The Grok ablation is informative because it tests a plausible protocol explanation directly. The selected baseline retained high coverage, and alternative schemas and labels did not produce a meaningful improvement. The low score is therefore reproducible under the tested protocol family. Attribution between model behavior and harness behavior remains limited to the variants that were run.

Calibration is a separate property from label accuracy. The local Qwen 4B result demonstrates that a probability interface can improve after split-safe calibration even when the selected labels remain unchanged. Jev, Qwen Flash, and Grok require validated probability outputs before they can be compared on Brier score, NLL, or ECE.

## 5. Limitations

- The pool covers objective typed decisions; open-ended legal, financial, human-preference, and expert-adjudicated tasks remain outside its scope.
- Accuracy is conditional on a resolved label. Unresolved execution and parsing outcomes are reported through coverage and the experiment artifacts.
- The newest eight-model results depend on the tested parser and execution protocol.
- The Grok analysis bounds model-versus-harness interpretation to the preregistered protocol family.

## 6. Conclusion

This study compared Jev with Grok, Qwen, and a broader set of contemporary model judges on a frozen pool of objectively labeled decisions. Jev reached 89.87% accuracy with complete blind coverage in the direct comparison. Qwen Flash performed better in that comparison, and Qwen3.8 Flash and Qwen 3.8 Max produced the strongest combination of accuracy and coverage in the extended wave. Grok performed substantially worse under the tested typed-judge protocol, and the preregistered ablation found no stronger replacement protocol.

The main result is a model comparison under a controlled task contract. The findings support continued evaluation of Jev as a reliable high-coverage judge and favor matched records, explicit labels, coverage, and validated confidence when selecting model judges. Future work will extend the same design to external objective sources with deterministic verification.
