Mechanically constraining an LLM orchestrator: control-plane authority and a same-family bias firewall

Pujan · Design Bakery

db-r-2026-003 · submitted 2026-07-22 · status pending

Owner gate & citation note. Design-descriptive working paper; no benchmark claim. Core references (Panickssery et al.; Prometheus 2; position-bias) are real; a small number of 2026 arXiv identifiers are AI-suggested and unverified and must be checked before this paper is marked approved.

Abstract

The orchestrator of a multi-agent system is its most privileged component: it plans, routes, retries, accepts or rejects worker output, and can terminate work. Giving those powers to a probabilistic model is fragile for two reasons — the model can favour its own or its family's generations, and it can argue past text-only constraints. This paper argues the correct response is not to debias or obey-train the orchestrator but to remove its authority: the model proposes, and a small deterministic controller owns state, permissions, retries, budgets, verification, and commits. We add a same-family bias firewall that makes a model's judgement of its own family advisory-only, and provenance-preserving blinding so evaluation sees opaque candidate IDs while audit keeps full provenance.

1. Problem

LLM evaluators can recognise and favour their own generations, sometimes without a disclosed source identity [1]; the effect can extend to same-family models and persist under objective rubrics [2]. Not every same-family preference is bias — stronger models may genuinely be better, and early self-preference measurements had confounds [3]. The safe design therefore does not auto-reject a model's own-family work; it disqualifies that model from being the final evaluator of it.

2. Architecture: propose vs. decide

  • LLM planner. Proposes task graphs, rosters, one of a fixed set of transitions, bounded retries, and abort/escalate recommendations. May decide what should happen next.
  • Deterministic controller. Validates the typed plan, owns the authoritative state machine, issues scoped capabilities, enforces budgets and termination, invokes verification, and decides commit eligibility. Decides whether it is permitted to happen. No natural-language reasoning in its path.

This mirrors durable-execution / workflow-controller practice, where a deterministic engine owns progress, retries, and state while application code (here, the model) only proposes the next step [4]. It is also least privilege [5] applied to the orchestrator.

3. The same-family bias firewall

Deterministic middleware sits between the orchestrator and every evaluation decision. On any accept / reject / rank / adjudicate / terminate where judge and candidate share a vendor family, the orchestrator verdict becomes advisory only and an independent non-family evaluation is required. The orchestrator cannot override this through natural-language reasoning — that is the point. Counter-bias checks: run each pair twice with order reversed (position bias is well documented [6]); have judges commit independently before seeing others; give judges the requirement, candidate, and test evidence but not other verdicts or vendor identities. Deterministic evidence dominates any model vote — a failing test plus a model saying "correct" is a fail.

4. Provenance-preserving blinding

Provenance is not discarded; it is split into two planes. A trusted provenance plane keeps artifact→model, signatures, and run IDs. An evaluation plane shows the deciding model only opaque candidate IDs, the rubric, and evidence. After the verdict is sealed, provenance is re-attached for audit and conflict/bias statistics. Prometheus 2 and similar open evaluators [7] serve as independent shadow judges and rubric critics — not as the sole truth oracle.

5. Testing the firewall

The firewall is validated on public bias benchmarks by evaluator family (gold-label accuracy, false-accept/reject, same-family preference delta, position-flip rate, disagreement with executable evidence): LLMBar [8], JudgeBench [9], self-preference measurement [2], Prometheus-Eval [7], and position-swap suites [6]. No model — or its family — both is a gate and scores whether that gate works.

6. Non-claims

  • Does not claim a deployed orchestrator-control plane; this is a design and its test plan.
  • Does not claim family bias is resolved — the direction of any Anthropic-authored gold is treated as unresolved pending objective third-party labels.
  • Invents no metrics; benchmark cells are empty until owner-approved.
