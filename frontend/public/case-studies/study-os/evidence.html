Evidence ledger

The pitch is simple. The claims should not be.

This page separates established learning-science findings from the product hypothesis. Study OS is not entitled to inherit a paper's effect size just because it uses a related mechanism.

Retrieval practice improves later learning outcomes.

Supported externally
Schwieren, Barenberg & Dutke, 2017
d = 0.56

Meta-analysis of retrieval practice in psychology learning and teaching. It extracted 72 effect sizes from 19 publications and reported a significant overall benefit of testing during learning.

DOI 10.1177/1475725717695149

Limit: this is not a Study OS trial and not specific to AI-assisted programming. It supports using retrieval as a learning signal instead of relying only on rereading or conversational fluency.

Retrieval can transfer, but transfer is not automatic.

Supported externally
Pan & Rickard, 2018
d = 0.40

Meta-analysis of 192 transfer effect sizes from 122 experiments and 67 articles, with 10,382 participants. The overall result favored testing over nontesting reexposure for transfer, while moderator analyses showed that the kind of transfer matters.

DOI 10.1037/bul0000151

Limit: transfer effects vary. Study OS therefore should test a changed problem directly rather than infer transfer from success on the practiced example.

Guidance should change as the learner becomes more capable.

Supported externally
Renkl, Atkinson & Große, 2004

Two experiments examined faded worked solution steps. The work supports progressively removing solution steps to bridge from studying examples toward independent problem solving.

DOI 10.1023/B:TRUC.0000021815.74806.F6

Paas & van Merriënboer, 2020

A review of cognitive-load methods describes the guidance-fading effect: worked examples can reduce load for novices, while redundant guidance can become counterproductive as expertise grows.

DOI 10.1177/0963721420922183

Limit: these findings support adaptive support and fading as instructional principles. They do not establish that Study OS can correctly diagnose when or how much help to remove.

Spacing and delayed checks matter for retention.

Supported externally
Murray, Horner & Göbel, 2025
g = 0.28

A meta-analysis of mathematics learning found a robust small-to-medium overall benefit for spaced versus massed practice across 27 studies and 53 effect sizes.

ERIC EJ1478558

Limit: this is mathematics-focused evidence. The durable design implication is narrower: a same-session success should not be the only retention check.

The product hypothesis is narrower than the marketing line.

Needs product validation
Study OS hypothesis

For AI-assisted technical learning, a system that identifies the learner's failure point, selects a targeted intervention, fades support, and checks independent recall, transfer, and delayed performance will produce better durable skill than an assistance loop optimized mainly for immediate task completion.

Not yet proven: the evidence above establishes useful mechanisms, not Study OS efficacy, superiority to existing tutors, or a commercial outcome.

What a credible Study OS benchmark should measure.

  • Independent success: can the learner solve without the original AI answer or transcript?
  • Transfer: can the learner solve a structurally related problem with changed surface details?
  • Delayed retention: can the learner retrieve and apply the skill after a preregistered delay?
  • Help dependence: how much prompting, hinting, or worked solution is required on the next attempt?
  • Diagnosis quality: did the system identify the actual failing step, or merely produce another plausible explanation?

A fair evaluation needs a comparator, preregistered outcomes, fixed task families, and reporting of failures as well as wins. Until that exists, the public copy should say what Study OS is designed to test, not claim measured learning gains.
