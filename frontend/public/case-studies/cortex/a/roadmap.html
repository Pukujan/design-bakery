Roadmap

Where this actually stands.

Three stages, in order. Each one is useful on its own, and each makes the next easier to build. This page is the honest record: it is updated when the status changes, including when it changes for the worse.

NowBuilding

Control what the agent can change

Approve a task once; every write is checked against that boundary before it lands. This is the part that runs today, and it is what makes unattended work safe rather than merely fast.

Next~6 months

Control what the agent can see

Sensitive material stops at the door. Names and values are masked on the way out and restored on the way back, and a task that cannot be separated from its sensitive context stays local. This is what unlocks regulated buyers.

Then~12 months

Prove it to an auditor

A clean record of every action taken and everything that left the machine, in the shape a compliance team already asks for.

Status · self-hosting

The kernel is being built by its own rules.

Every module ships as a pull request authored by the broker's own identity and approved through the gates it will use in production. The thing that will build trustworthy software is, right now, building itself that way.

What is proven, and at what level

status as of 2026-07-26

The headline counts are module-level claims and they are accurate at that level. They do not say the assembled system works, and an earlier version of this page did not draw that line clearly enough. Drawing it is the whole thesis, so it belongs here.

  • Composition was broken. On 2026-07-25/26 an end-to-end wiring audit found 15 failures between modules that each passed their own tests: an authorization call passing one argument where three were required, a caller reading a field that does not exist, and the durable receipt component invoked zero times during a full composed run. The repository's own words for it are "units green, chain hollow."
  • Since fixed and independently verified: approval receipts now mint, check and consume as separate steps, confirmed by a direct-import run against the kernel with no test pack and no adapter, 6/6 refusal behaviours.
  • Still open, and material: an approval is not yet re-bound to its content at apply time, so approved content can be substituted before the write. Sealed test packs also cannot yet distinguish whether the unit or the test harness implements a behaviour.
  • Kernel completion: 2 of 10 critical-path steps closed.

Do not read the illustrated broker path on this page as operational. It is the design and the target, not a description of a running production system.

The reason this section exists: on 2026-07-26 three models from different vendors, given the source and the contract but never the tests, independently raised 11 findings, 10 distinct, of which 6 are code defects and 4 are contract ambiguities. A 26/26 green sealed-holdout suite had caught none of them. One is fixed so far, and it is the only one with a regression test. An audit of that claim also found the assessors were given a list of attack families, so 7 of the 11 map onto a prompted angle rather than an invented one. Tests that pass are not evidence that a system works, and neither is a headline number that has not been audited. Both findings are the case study.
