Case study · research system

An evidence-gated harness for trustworthy autonomous AI.

A research system that tackles a hard question — can AI agents build and verify software autonomously without you having to trust their word? — and answers it with deterministic gates instead of faith.

My role Sole architect and director — architecture, verification methodology, orchestration.
Status Solo research system, active development. Not a shipped product.
Implementation AI agents, working inside a harness built to keep them honest.

Honest framing, up front

This is agent-orchestrated engineering. The contribution is the systems design and the verification discipline — not hand-written novel algorithms. Every number below is tagged by how well it is backed, and the tags are the point.

01 · the problem

Fluent, confident, and wrong is the dangerous combination.

Most agentic systems answer this by putting a human in front of every step. Cortex asks whether you can replace trust with mechanism.

What an agent will tell you

"The tests pass. I verified it end-to-end."

Both are things an agent will say whether or not they are true. Fluency is not evidence, and a confident report is the cheapest thing a model produces.

What the kernel requires instead

A machine-checkable artifact, or it did not happen.

A frozen contract, a sealed probe suite the builder never saw, killed mutants, and a deterministic checker's verdict. No step advances on an assertion.

02 · the approach

Three principles, each made mechanical rather than aspirational.

01

Contract-first

Freeze what "done" means — as a testable observable — before any code is written. An agent cannot move goalposts it cannot see.

02

Objective verification, not judgment

Where possible a deterministic checker, not another LLM, decides pass/fail. Gates that could be gamed get an adversarial holdout whose whole job is to try to game them.

03

Independence

The agent that builds is never the agent that verifies, and the verifier's tests are sealed from the builder — so it cannot fix-to-the-test.

03 · verification path

How a unit of work earns the word "done".

Nothing is accepted on an agent's report. A work unit advances only by passing gates that a machine, not a model, decides.

work-unit lifecycle · builder ≠ verifier deterministic verdict 
flowchart TD
  C["Contract frozen<br/>(testable observable)"] --> B["Builder agent<br/>writes implementation"]
  C --> H["Verifier agent authors<br/>SEALED holdout probes"]
  H -. "never shared with builder" .-> B
  B --> R{"Run sealed probes<br/>against blind build"}
  H --> R
  R -- "fail" --> F["Defect recorded<br/>· returned to builder"]
  F --> B
  R -- "pass" --> M{"Inject mutants:<br/>are they all killed?"}
  M -- "survivor" --> W["Probe suite too weak<br/>· strengthen and re-run"]
  W --> H
  M -- "all killed" --> D["Deterministic checker<br/>decides pass/fail"]
  D --> V[["Verdict + evidence ledger"]]
  F --> V

The two independent authors are the load-bearing part. Because the probe suite is written from the frozen contract and sealed before the build exists, a builder cannot tune to it — and because every injected mutant must die before a verdict counts, a suite that merely passes is not yet trusted.

This is not a hypothetical failure mode

The sealed holdouts repeatedly caught real defects the builder's own green test suite missed — including a floor-bypass defect that a 41/41-passing test-driven suite sailed straight past, and an owner-approval-gate bypass on a resume path. "Building to the test" was caught empirically here, not asserted.

An objective evaluation harness

Committed

3,528 verification records across five lanes — tool-calling (synthetic plus live cross-family data graded by BFCL's own checker), coding with hidden holdout tests, defensive security, research faithfulness, and architecture. Each is decided by a deterministic checker, with "judge_in_verdict_path": false recorded in the committed ledger.

Caveats. A local tool-calling checker agrees with BFCL's canonical checker at ~99.93% at the report level — the full row-level comparison is not committed. 3,528 is the Stage-2 snapshot; the catalog has since grown past it (~5,800). ~41 frozen checker tests are committed, though a "48" figure appears in summary prose.

LLM-judge calibration — including finding that the gold my own pipeline produced was vendor-biased

Committed

I measured judge reliability with Cohen's κ across versioned rubrics. A richer rubric lifted a capable judge from κ ≈0.62 to 0.92 with the model held constant — while the same rubric regressed a small 4B model from ≈0.60 to 0.52. The durable asset is the rubric, not the model.

The sharpest result cuts against the model family I had chosen to build on. My gold labels were authored by Anthropic models; on those labels, Anthropic judges agreed at weighted-κ 0.95 versus non-Anthropic 0.50 — an explicit self-preference flag. A purpose-built independent judge (Prometheus) agreed at κ = 0.000, which the audit itself names as the clean family-bias test to trust over the gentler family-mean.

The disposition that follows. The labels "have signal but are not vendor-neutral", so 0 records were promoted to training gold, and the judge-free objective harness above exists precisely because this bias is — in the project's own words — unresolved without third-party gold. It is partly capability rather than pure vendor: the strongest non-Anthropic judge reached 0.75.

Sealed-holdout verification with mutation testing

Committed

Independent probe suites, authored sealed and run against blind builds the test author never saw. Across kernel modules every injected mutant was killed — contract-engine 10/10, human-control 13/13, broker 6/6, model_dispatch 5/5 — and the holdouts repeatedly caught real defects the builder's own green suite missed (a floor-bypass past a 41/41-passing suite; an owner-approval-gate bypass on a resume path, 16/21 probes deterministic on double-run).

Multi-model routing calibration

Committed

A cheap model passed only 14/41 (34%) first-pass against a frozen oracle suite — with failures all logic-class, not interface crashes — while a stronger model repaired every failure to 41/41. That is the empirical basis for risk-tiered routing: expensive models run only where a task's measured difficulty demands them.

Hybrid retrieval (RAG)

Self-measured

BM25 + vector reciprocal-rank-fusion over the project's own technical-doc corpus, tuned against a chunk-level graded eval (nDCG@5 ≈0.46 → 0.65; chunk_recall@5 0.467 → 0.667; chunk_mrr 0.425 → 0.503). Scope-packing cut retrieval context by ~96% on the golden-set mean.

Caveats. The graded eval is agent-authored and pending human review, n=17 (a one-query swing is ≈6%), one companion doc records a slightly different nDCG for the same run, and these live in prose rather than a committed ledger. But the gate worked: three candidate improvements (KP-miner, chunking-v2, an ms-marco reranker) were rejected on the eval's evidence; only the fourth shipped.
05 · what it demonstrates

Four capabilities, in order of how rare they are.

Evaluation engineering

Objective benchmarks, statistical calibration, adversarial verification. The rarest and most transferable skill in the set.

A verification instinct

Treating "the model said so" as worthless, and building the mechanisms that make autonomous work auditable.

AI systems architecture

A coherent multi-agent system with explicit authority boundaries and fail-closed behaviour.

Pragmatic AI engineering

RAG, retrieval tuning, cost and latency optimisation, model routing.

06 · honest limitations

What this is not.

Solo, research-grade, not production-hardened. Metrics are self-measured on this system and not externally audited — which is exactly why they carry a Committed or Self-measured tag above rather than being presented uniformly.

The objective lanes and calibration κ are machine-backed; the retrieval and scope-pack numbers live in agent-authored eval docs pending review. The value here is a demonstration of how I approach trustworthy AI — not a claim of production scale. The methodology and the judgment are the transferable parts.

07 · how this document was checked

The write-up went through the same treatment as the system.

To avoid the exact failure this project studies, this case study was itself put through a cross-vendor adversarial audit — a panel of non-Anthropic models (GLM-5.2 / Zhipu and Gemini 3.5 Flash / Google) tasked to refute it against the repository's actual source files.

They found a real problem

An earlier draft cited the milder vendor-bias number and omitted the sharper self-preference finding — the 0.95 versus 0.50 split now shown above. That correction, and others, are folded in. A write-up that hid its worst number would have failed its own thesis.
