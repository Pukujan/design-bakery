CX-Flow · execution control

How the Cortex kernel decides.

Every proposed operation follows one path: a request carrying declared authority, a deny-by-default check, a policy gate evaluated where the concrete effect is known, brokered execution through a sole writer, and a receipt that binds what was asked, what was decided, and what changed. The diagrams below read top to bottom.

01 · decision path

Request to receipt.

An actor never writes state directly. It proposes an operation; the kernel decides whether that proposal is permitted to become an effect. Denials and approvals both leave evidence.

proposal → verdict → effect deny by default 
flowchart TD
  A["Actor proposes an operation"] --> B{"Explicit authority sufficient?"}
  B -- "no" --> D["Deny by default"]
  B -- "yes" --> P{"Policy gate:<br/>constraints satisfied?"}
  P -- "no" --> D
  P -- "yes" --> BR["Brokered execution<br/>(tool_broker)"]
  BR --> W["Sole-writer commit"]
  W --> R["Execution receipt"]
  D --> R2["Refusal receipt"]
  R --> AUD[["Audit trail"]]
  R2 --> AUD

The two decision diamonds are the whole boundary: authority (is this actor allowed this class of operation on this target?) and policy (are current constraints satisfied at the point where the real effect is known?). Failing either resolves to a refusal — never to a silent write.

Explicit authority

A declared grant whose operation and target scope cover the request. Missing, unknown, invalid, or insufficient authority resolves to a refusal.

Policy gate

Current constraints are evaluated at the boundary where the concrete effect is known — not earlier, when only intent is visible.

Sole-writer commit

Controlled writes are concentrated in tool_broker, so authorization cannot be bypassed by a parallel path.

Receipts

Evidence connecting the proposal, the authorization decision, the broker outcome, and the resulting state — for both approvals and refusals.

02 · seven mechanisms

The complete control path.

The decision above is enforced by seven mechanisms in sequence. Each one narrows what a proposal can become before it is allowed to touch state.

ordered enforcement pipeline seven mechanisms 
flowchart TD
  M1["1 · Request envelope"] --> M2["2 · Deny by default"]
  M2 --> M3["3 · Explicit authority"]
  M3 --> M4["4 · Policy gate"]
  M4 --> M5["5 · Brokered tools"]
  M5 --> M6["6 · Sole-writer commit"]
  M6 --> M7["7 · Execution receipts"]

Read as a pipeline: structure the request, refuse by default, require declared authority, evaluate policy at the effect boundary, route execution through the broker, keep the broker the sole writer, and return receipts. Full definitions live in the technical specification.
