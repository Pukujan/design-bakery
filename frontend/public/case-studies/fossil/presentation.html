The problem

You remember the decision.
You don't remember how you got there.

That gap becomes expensive when the work spans days, agents, sources, experiments and architecture changes.

FOSSIL is built around one idea: do not ask a model to reconstruct history when the history can be preserved.

What changes when knowledge changes

Old conclusions do not disappear. Their status changes.

supported · thenSQLite is the canonical store
new observationA requirement breaks the old premise
supported · nowUse the durable append-only core
stale_pending_reviewA dependent prototype needs review

“Needs review” is not the same claim as “false.” That distinction is the point.

The epistemic layer

A project can keep more than decisions.

Claims. Assumptions. Questions. Arguments. Observations. Experiments. Evidence. Decisions.

Support can coexist with challenge. A later claim can supersede an earlier one without deleting it. A changed premise can make its dependents stale.

That is why “memory” feels too small as the category. FOSSIL is trying to preserve the project's epistemic history.

Adversarial review

Three claims we refuse to make.

“It recreates everything.”

No. It reconstructs what was actually captured. Missing private reasoning stays missing.

“A hashed source is true.”

No. A hash gives integrity. Authority, independence, timeliness and applicability still need judgment.

“The model is confident, so the claim is current.”

No. Confidence, retrieval rank and agent agreement are signals, not truth state.

Internal benchmark · not third-party validation

Small internal benchmark, 2026-08-10. The lesson is not “perfect score.” The lesson is that retrieval was not allowed to rewrite history.
