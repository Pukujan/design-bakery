Research behind the pitch

Here is what the framing is based on.

The main page is meant to be easy to read. This page is where the research sits. None of these studies prove that FOSSIL will succeed as a product. They do show that developers lose rationale, spend time rebuilding context, and need more than a one-line record of a decision.

Rationale gets scattered through the work.

A study of developer chat found rationale in a meaningful share of messages. More recent work on commit rationale also describes the reasoning as spread across commits, issues and pull requests.

SANER 2018 · 2026 preprint

What this supports: the reasoning exists in day-to-day artifacts, but not as one clean history.

When developers ask “why?”, they are asking for a lot.

Al-Safwan and colleagues broke commit rationale into 15 components. That includes goals, alternatives, validation, dependencies, constraints, timing and side effects.

Journal of Systems and Software, 2022

What this supports: a final decision note does not necessarily contain the detail people need later.

Rebuilding the story is not the same as preserving it.

A 2025 study compared LLM-generated architecture rationale with human expert rationale. The models recovered many relevant points, but also introduced arguments that were not part of the expert rationale.

2025 architecture rationale study

What this supports: a useful generated explanation and a faithful historical record are different things.

Capturing rationale has always had a friction problem.

Architecture rationale research has repeatedly found time and tooling to be barriers. Unakite is a useful example of work showing that better capture tooling can reduce the effort of recording tradeoffs.

JSS 2006 · UIST 2019

What we are not claiming.

FOSSIL cannot recover thoughts that were never captured. A hash does not make a claim true. Memory systems are useful. Other decision and rationale products exist. Internal FOSSIL benchmarks are not independent proof of general accuracy.
