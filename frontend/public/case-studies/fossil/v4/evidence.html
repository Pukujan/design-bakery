Evidence ledger

The public story is sales copy. These are the receipts.

This page keeps the supporting research and the limits of each claim separate from the main FOSSIL pitch. The product claim is narrow: FOSSIL preserves captured decision lineage; it does not recover reasoning that was never recorded.

Rationale gets fragmented.

Development artifacts and communication contain rationale, but not as one clean decision history.

Research on design rationale in developer communication found rationale present in a substantial share of chat messages, and recent work on commit rationale emphasizes that rationale is spread across commits, issues and pull requests.

SANER 2018 DOI · 2026 commit-rationale preprint

Limit: these studies support fragmentation and rationale-in-communication. They do not prove FOSSIL's market adoption.

Developers need more than a one-line why.

“Why?” often means goals, alternatives, constraints, validation, dependencies, side effects and timing.

Al-Safwan et al. decomposed commit rationale into 15 components and reported repeated rationale-seeking behavior among developers.

JSS 2022 DOI

Limit: commit rationale is narrower than the broader FOSSIL target of research and epistemic lineage.

Reconstruction is not provenance.

An LLM can generate plausible design rationale without being a faithful record of the historical reasoning chain.

A 2025 study comparing LLM-generated architectural design rationale against human-expert rationale found low precision despite high recall, including arguments not present in expert rationale.

ADR generation study

Limit: model performance changes quickly. The durable point is conceptual: generated rationale and preserved provenance are different artifacts.

Capture friction matters.

People often do not document rationale because it costs time and lacks good tooling.

A long-running architecture-rationale survey found time/budget, standards and tooling as barriers; Unakite showed structured capture can reduce tradeoff-capture cost in a study setting.

JSS 2006 DOI · UIST 2019 DOI

Limit: older studies and another product do not imply FOSSIL will win. They support the problem and the value of easier capture.

Claims deliberately not made.

Not claimed:

FOSSIL perfectly recreates every human thought; memory is useless; no competing products exist; a hash proves a claim is true; internal benchmarks prove general accuracy.
