Evidence ledger

The marketing claims should survive somebody trying to break them.

This page separates external evidence, FOSSIL's own internal benchmark, and claims we deliberately do not make. It is not a literature review and it is not proof that FOSSIL is commercially successful.

Claim 01

Resuming programming work often requires rebuilding context.

Supported · external
Parnin & Rugaber, ICPC 2009

Exploratory analysis of 10,000 recorded programming sessions. Only 10% resumed coding in less than a minute; 7% involved no navigation elsewhere before editing.

DOI 10.1109/ICPC.2009.5090030

Limit: this is interruption/task-resumption evidence, not a direct measure of architecture-decision forgetting and not an AI-era study.

Claim 02

Developers repeatedly need the rationale behind code changes, and difficult searches can take substantial time.

Supported · external
Al-Safwan et al., Journal of Systems and Software, 2022

Developers decomposed commit rationale into 15 components. Most reported seeking rationale multiple times a week or more; hard cases could take more than 20 minutes.

DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2022.111320

Limit: commit rationale is narrower than FOSSIL's broader epistemic lineage (research, assumptions, experiments and decisions).

Claim 03

Manual rationale documentation has adoption friction.

Supported · external
Tang, Babar, Gorton & Han, Journal of Systems and Software, 2006

Among 81 valid survey responses, 60.5% cited lack of time/budget as a reason not to document design rationale; 42% cited no standards and 29.6% no suitable tool.

DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2006.04.029

Limit: a 2006 architecture survey does not tell us today's exact adoption rates. We use it as evidence of long-standing capture friction, not a current market-size statistic.

Claim 04

Reducing the cost of capturing decision rationale can improve later understanding.

Supported · external experiment
Liu et al., UIST 2019 — Unakite

The authors report 45% lower cost for capturing tradeoff-related information and about a 3× speedup for a subsequent developer understanding the saved trade-offs.

DOI 10.1145/3332165.3347908

Limit: Unakite is a different product and study setup. Its results support the rationale-capture problem and value of structured context, not FOSSIL's performance.

Claim 05

Long-term conversational memory remains difficult even when assistants have memory components or long context.

Supported · external benchmark
Wu et al., ICLR 2025 — LongMemEval

500 curated questions test extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention. The paper reports about a 30% accuracy drop for commercial assistants and long-context LLMs across sustained interactions.

ICLR 2025 paper

Limit: LongMemEval tests conversational memory. FOSSIL is not claiming that memory is useless or that this benchmark directly measures provenance.

Claim 06

Putting evidence inside a long context does not guarantee robust use of that evidence.

Supported · external benchmark
Liu et al., TACL 2024 — Lost in the Middle

Performance varied substantially with evidence position. In multi-document QA, GPT-3.5-Turbo could drop by more than 20 percentage points when relevant information was poorly positioned.

DOI 10.1162/tacl_a_00638

Limit: model and benchmark results age quickly. The durable claim here is narrower: long context capacity is not identical to robust historical reconstruction.

Claim 07

FOSSIL's lineage layer can prevent a retrieval miss from silently becoming historical authority.

Internal evidence · small benchmark
FOSSIL internal answer-reliability proof, 2026-08-10

Initial six-case run: 5/6. A stale dependent claim was absent from top-k; the answerer selected an unrelated supported claim with confidence 1.0. After deterministic lineage endpoint resolution, the unchanged six cases scored 6/6 and the missed case returned current_state_unresolved with exact citation.

Limit: six cases, developed and run by the project author. This is regression evidence, not independent validation and not a claim of general 100% accuracy.

Rejected claims

Claims kept off the page after adversarial review.

“FOSSIL perfectly recreates why a decision happened.”

Rejected. It cannot recover reasoning that was never captured. Current wording: it makes captured evidence and recorded lineage inspectable.

“Memory doesn't work.”

Rejected. Memory is useful for recall and personalization. The product distinction is that recall does not itself guarantee provenance.

“No other product solves this.”

Rejected pending a proper competitive survey. The public site describes FOSSIL's contract rather than making an exclusivity claim.

“A cryptographic hash proves a claim is true.”

Rejected. It proves integrity of the captured bytes, not truth, relevance or source quality.
