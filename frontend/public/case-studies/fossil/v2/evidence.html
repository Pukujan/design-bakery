Evidence ledger

The marketing claims should survive somebody trying to break them.

This page separates external research, market estimates, analyst forecasts, current product evidence and FOSSIL's own internal benchmark. It is not proof that FOSSIL is commercially successful, and it is not an exhaustive literature or competitor review.

Claim 01

Resuming programming work often requires rebuilding context.

Supported · external study
Parnin & Rugaber, ICPC 2009

Exploratory analysis of 10,000 recorded programming sessions. Only 10% resumed coding in less than a minute; 7% involved no navigation elsewhere before editing.

DOI 10.1109/ICPC.2009.5090030

Limit: interruption/task-resumption evidence is not a direct measure of architecture-decision forgetting and predates current AI coding tools.

Claim 02

Developers repeatedly need the rationale behind code changes.

Supported · external study
Al-Safwan et al., Journal of Systems and Software, 2022

Developers decomposed commit rationale into 15 components. Most reported seeking rationale multiple times a week or more; difficult cases could take more than 20 minutes.

DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2022.111320

Limit: commit rationale is narrower than FOSSIL's broader research and epistemic lineage.

Claim 03

Manual rationale capture has adoption friction.

Supported · external survey
Tang, Babar, Gorton & Han, JSS 2006

Among 81 valid responses, 60.5% cited lack of time/budget as a reason not to document design rationale; 42% cited no standards and 29.6% no suitable tool.

DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2006.04.029

Limit: this is a 2006 architecture survey, so it is evidence of long-standing friction, not a current adoption rate.

Claim 04

Cheaper structured capture can improve later understanding.

Supported · external experiment
Liu et al., UIST 2019 — Unakite

The authors report 45% lower cost for capturing trade-off information and roughly a 3× speedup for a subsequent developer understanding the saved trade-offs.

DOI 10.1145/3332165.3347908

Limit: Unakite is a different product and study setup. It supports the rationale-capture problem, not FOSSIL's performance.

Claim 05

Long-term conversational memory remains difficult.

Supported · external benchmark
Wu et al., ICLR 2025 — LongMemEval

500 curated questions cover extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention. The paper reports about a 30% accuracy drop for commercial assistants and long-context LLMs over sustained interactions.

ICLR 2025 paper

Limit: this tests conversational memory. It does not directly test provenance or FOSSIL.

Claim 06

Long context does not guarantee robust use of evidence.

Supported · external benchmark
Liu et al., TACL 2024 — Lost in the Middle

Performance varied substantially with evidence position. In multi-document QA, GPT-3.5-Turbo could drop by more than 20 percentage points when relevant information was poorly positioned.

DOI 10.1162/tacl_a_00638

Limit: model results age quickly. The narrower durable point is that context capacity is not identical to historical reconstruction.

Claim 07

FOSSIL's lineage layer can stop a retrieval miss from becoming historical authority.

Internal evidence · small benchmark
FOSSIL answer-reliability proof, 2026-08-10

Initial six-case run: 5/6. A stale dependent claim was absent from top-k and the answerer selected an unrelated supported claim with confidence 1.0. After deterministic lineage endpoint resolution, the unchanged cases scored 6/6 and the missed case returned current_state_unresolved with exact citation.

Limit: six cases, built and run by the project author. This is regression evidence, not independent validation or general 100% accuracy.

Claim 08

AI adoption in software development is high while trust is materially lower.

Supported · external survey
Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025

84% use or plan to use AI tools in development; 51% of professional developers use them daily. Forty-six percent actively distrust AI accuracy versus 33% who trust it, and 66% cite “almost right” answers as the biggest AI frustration.

2025 survey · AI section

Limit: a voluntary developer survey shows adoption and sentiment, not FOSSIL's addressable market.

Claim 09

AI coding is already a meaningful software-spend category.

Supported · market estimate
Menlo Ventures, 2025 State of Generative AI in the Enterprise

Menlo estimates $37B in enterprise generative-AI spend in 2025, including $19B at the application layer and $4.0B in AI coding products.

Menlo Ventures report

Limit: this is a venture firm's survey and bottoms-up market model, not audited industry revenue and not a FOSSIL TAM calculation.

Claim 10

Enterprise software is expected to become more agentic.

Supported · analyst forecast
Gartner, August 2025

Gartner forecasts that 40% of enterprise applications will include integrated task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from less than 5% at the time of the forecast.

Gartner press release

Limit: this is a forecast. It may be wrong; the public page labels it as a forecast.

Claim 11

Persistent agent memory/context is already sold as a separate paid layer.

Supported · current product evidence
Mem0

Current self-serve plans include $19/month Starter and $249/month Pro, with graph memory on Pro.

Mem0 pricing

Zep

Current self-serve pricing starts at $125/month Flex and $375/month Flex Plus; plans include Memory MCP seats and temporal-memory features.

Zep pricing

Limit: posted prices show that the category is monetized, not vendor revenue, customer count or product-market fit.

Claim 12

Decision rationale and institutional decision history are established product categories too.

Supported · production + OSS landscape
Ardoq Architecture Records

Ardoq supports rationale, alternatives, status, approval, review dates, related decisions and past decisions that are no longer current.

Ardoq documentation

Cloverpop

Cloverpop describes a decision system of record that captures what was decided, why, by whom and what happened, alongside a decision graph and governance.

Cloverpop platform

adrkit · open source

adrkit describes itself as decision memory for human- and agent-authored plans, with machine-readable ADRs, CI enforcement and an MCP server that retrieves rejected and superseded decisions.

GitHub · mbeacom/adrkit

Limit: these systems overlap materially with parts of FOSSIL. We do not claim FOSSIL has no competitors. The narrower distinction being tested is source-grounded epistemic lineage across evidence, claims, assumptions, opposition, dependencies and state changes.

Rejected

Claims kept off the page after adversarial review.

“FOSSIL perfectly recreates why a decision happened.”

Rejected. It cannot recover reasoning that was never captured.

“Memory doesn't work.”

Rejected. Memory is useful for recall and personalization. Recall simply does not guarantee provenance.

“FOSSIL has no competitors.”

Rejected. Memory infrastructure, ADR tooling, architecture-record systems and decision-intelligence products all overlap with parts of the problem. This review did not find a public product demonstrating the exact same full contract, but absence of evidence is not proof of uniqueness.

“A cryptographic hash proves a claim is true.”

Rejected. It proves integrity of captured bytes, not truth, relevance or source quality.
