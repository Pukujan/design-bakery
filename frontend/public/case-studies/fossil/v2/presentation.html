The problem

You remember the decision.
You don't remember how you got there.

That gap gets expensive when the work spans days, agents, sources, experiments and architecture changes.

FOSSIL starts with one rule: do not ask a model to reconstruct history when the important parts of that history can be preserved.

The epistemic layer

A project can keep more than decisions.

Claims. Assumptions. Questions. Arguments. Observations. Experiments. Evidence. Decisions.

Support can coexist with challenge. A later claim can supersede an earlier one without deleting it. A changed premise can make its dependents stale.

That is why “memory” feels too small as the category. FOSSIL is trying to preserve the project's epistemic history.

Adversarial review

Three claims we refuse to make.

“It recreates everything.”

No. It reconstructs what was captured. Missing reasoning stays missing.

“A hashed source is true.”

No. A hash gives integrity. Authority and relevance remain separate questions.

“FOSSIL has no competitors.”

No. Memory systems, ADR tools and decision-intelligence products overlap materially.
