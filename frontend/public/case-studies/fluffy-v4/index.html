Fluffy System · Design Gallery V4

Same product. Different worlds.

Coding agents can already produce a polished landing page in minutes. Fluffy gives them the harder, upstream job: research the product, the buyer, the market and the proof first, then render several genuinely different directions for a human to judge. V4 is the eight-direction gallery that came out of that loop.

The problem

AI made marketing pages cheap. It didn't make them worth reading.

The hard part is deciding what deserves to be on the page: who the buyer is, what they are struggling with, what they use today, where the market leaves them short, which proof matters, and what visual world makes the product feel distinct. An agent that skips that work produces copy that is fluent, generic and about the system instead of the buyer.

“The agent has to understand the buyer before it gets to decorate the page.”

How Fluffy works

Research, positioning, copy and art direction as one creative job.

You point Fluffy at the product source and the place the marketing should live. The workflow is written for coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and others) and asks them to do the homework in order.

Read the real product

Docs, PRs, incidents, usage and current limitations, not just the README headline.

Find the buyer's words

How the audience describes the problem in the wild, and what they use today.

Make the pain concrete

Credible market data and external research, with sources kept alongside the claim.

Branch the creative

Several positioning angles and at least 20 brand and interaction references per major direction.

Render, then publish

Working HTML candidates, an anti-AI-pattern copy pass, and a review gallery.

HUMAN IN THE LOOP The final taste call stays with the owner: keep, combine, revise or delete any candidate.
Four galleries in two days

From eight polished candidates to eight different worlds.

Every gallery was published as an unlisted, noindex review surface on this site. The pilot products were Project Assurance Modules (public) and Research Assurance (a private product, so it is never linked).

What V4 taught

Concept art has to survive the trip into production.

V4 looked right as concept art and went wrong in the build. The failures were concrete, so the fix is a pipeline rather than another round of patches.

✕What went wrong

  • Detail pages embedded large data:image base64 payloads inside CSS.
  • Gallery previews were, at one point, whole pages rendered inside scaled iframes.
  • A replacement Study Partner image fixed the file format but not the visual direction.
  • Work was judged from code changes instead of repeated browser screenshots.
one full-page image→CSS background→unrelated HTML

✓The rebuild pipeline

  • Important copy, buttons, navigation and cards stay real HTML.
  • Generated imagery supplies scenes and supporting art as normal image files.
  • Phone layouts are rearranged on purpose, not shrunk.
  • Nothing is called finished until desktop and mobile screenshots match the reference.
reference→separate assets→real HTML/CSS→mobile layout→screenshot loop
Status · ongoing

Rebuilding one direction at a time.

The rebuild is planned in the repo handoff. The first milestone is the Study Partner page, matched against its visual reference on desktop and designed intentionally for phones. The other directions follow in order, without forcing them into one template, and the gallery moves to real image thumbnails at the end.

Claim boundaries

What this page does not claim.

No usage or conversion numbers

This page reports no traffic, usage or conversion results for Fluffy. Any stats that appear inside design reference mockups are placeholder design content, not results.

Candidates, not a launched site

The gallery pages are unlisted review candidates. None of them has been promoted as the final marketing page for its product.

Private sources stay private

Research Assurance is a private product. Its pages describe it, but nothing here links to its repository.

Screenshots show today's state

Thumbnails were captured from the live pages as they are now, including art that is known to be wrong and scheduled for rebuild.
