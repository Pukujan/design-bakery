You finished the research. Are you actually ready to build?

Starting a repo is easy. Starting the right project is harder. PAM is a versioned preflight methodology for the moment a researched idea is about to become architecture, issues, code, benchmarks, and release claims.

A real PAM failure

The plan looked complete. The research behind one decision was not.

A real adopter exposed a loophole in PAM's first build-vs-reuse method. An agent could write a plausible candidate register without proving that it searched concrete alternatives deeply enough.

The failure was not “the agent forgot a checkbox.” The checkbox could pass while the important work had not happened.

What PAM changes

Make the project earn its shape before the backlog hardens around it.

PAM does not decide whether your research is intellectually complete. Once you decide to projectize, it makes the applicable planning and evidence work explicit enough to revisit, review, and hand off.

01Declare the project

What is the current outcome? What is out of scope?

02Route what applies

Planning, reuse, benchmark integrity, continuity, provenance, and more only where relevant.

03Close gaps with evidence

Tests, files, search receipts, exact revisions, reviewed decisions.

04Freeze the preflight

The project can say which methodology revision it actually followed.

05Build with a handoff

Future humans and agents inherit current state, not only old conversation context.

Market thesis

The next bottleneck is not generating more code.

DORA's 2025 research describes AI as an amplifier. It can increase throughput, but weak foundations can turn speed into instability or accelerate low-value work.

As implementation gets cheaper, project readiness becomes more valuable.

What PAM is trying to own

The repeatable transition from researched idea to project: scope, applicable methodology, evidence expectations, handoff, and project-specific closure.

What it deliberately does not own

The truth of the research conclusion, runtime authorization, every project decision, or a universal “verified” stamp.

The pitch

Before you build the project, make sure you actually have one.

PAM is a public, pre-v1 experiment in versioned project preflight. The promise is not perfect planning. It is a harder thing to fake: knowing what you meant to build, what you checked first, and what evidence will count when someone says the work is done.
