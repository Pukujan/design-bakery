01 / Why this exists

Page generation got fast. Figuring out what the page should say still takes judgment.

“Who is this actually for?”

A product repo can explain architecture beautifully and still leave the buyer undefined.

“What are they doing today?”

The real competitor might be a spreadsheet, ChatGPT, an internal ritual, or doing nothing. Positioning starts there.

“Why should this look like us?”

A gradient and five cards can make almost any AI product feel familiar. Brand research gives the design a world of its own.

02 / The working set

The research has to change the creative brief.

01

Product truth

Read primary sources and find the real incident that explains why the project exists.

02

Buyer language

Search communities, issues, reviews, and practitioner writing for how people describe the problem.

03

Market gap

Map direct tools, status quo, manual work, buying trigger, and the remaining friction.

04

Brand territory

Study at least 20 useful references for major work. Build three genuinely different worlds.

05

Rendered options

Generate complete pages. Let the owner compare the finished work instead of approving every intermediate thought.

04 / Market timing

AI already increased the volume. That makes sameness easier to notice.

Ahrefs surveyed 879 marketers in 2025. 87% said they use AI to help create content, and AI users published 42% more content per month in the sample. A separate Journal of Business Research paper found authenticity penalties when consumers believed emotional marketing messages were AI-authored.
